    Results by level

    Why is L3 less accurate than no log?

    L3 removes almost every conflict, yet solves fewer tasks than L0. Three findings explain the gap.

      Conflicts between agents

      A conflict is a write to a record, or of a new item, that a teammate had already written. It comes in two kinds. A duplicate went through a second time, so the world changed twice; AppWorld's tests check that nothing extra changed, so a duplicate can fail the task. A collision failed because the work was already done (such as "already downloaded"), so nothing changed.

      Solved: the share of those task-runs that passed all of AppWorld's tests. A lower share for task-runs with a duplicate than for those with no conflict marks duplicates as harmful; a share close to it marks collisions as harmless. This compares task-runs, so harder tasks with more writes also weigh on it.

      Accuracy

      A task-run counts as solved only if every one of AppWorld's tests passes: the expected changes, nothing else changed, and the right answer for questions.

      Time, tokens and concurrency

      All levels ran side by side in the same jobs, so their times saw the same load. Time is the team's wall time per task.

      Overlap: share of the team's time with two or more agents working. Parallelism: the agents' summed working time over the team's time (1 means one agent at a time, 3 means all three throughout).

      How agents used the log

      Per task-run. Refusals are counted by the rule that fired; a refusal chain is a run of refusals with no accepted post or write in between.

      Level against level, task by task

      Each task-run is compared with the same task in the same job at the other level. p is a two-sided sign-flip test; the time interval is a 95% bootstrap interval.
