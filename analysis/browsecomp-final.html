BrowseComp-Plus with gpt-oss-20b

Our first runs on the paper’s second benchmark: hard multi-hop questions answered by searching a fixed collection of about 100,000 web pages. The document collection is read-only, so a team can share what it found only by talking. This page covers the setup, the checks, a single-agent run on the paper’s 100 questions, every setup on the first 10, a pilot that splits the collection between agents so that they have to share, and why the agents still didn’t talk.

How we run it

We follow the scaling paper’s released code wherever our hardware allows. The questions, the document collection and the search index are the ones the paper used.

Questions
The paper’s sample of 100 from BrowseComp-Plus’s 830: 10 from each of 10 topics (people, geography, sports, politics and so on).
Documents
100,195 web pages, searched only through the tools below. No live web.
Search
The paper’s Qwen3-Embedding-4B index. The query is embedded by a second model server on the same GPU and every page is ranked by similarity.
Tools
search_documents (top 5 hits with a snippet), retrieve_document (a page, 20,000 characters at a time), done (answer and confidence).
Prompts
The paper’s BrowseComp prompts, word for word, asking for an explanation, an exact answer and a confidence.
Grading
The paper’s grader prompt, answered by gpt-oss-20b at temperature 0. Each answer is also checked against the gold answer by string match.
Hardware
One L40 per job: gpt-oss-20b in 70% of its memory, the embedding model in 25%.

The full-text search results only fit Gemini’s 1M-token context; with gpt-oss-20b’s 128K a few searches would fill it. Comparisons between our own setups are clean (same questions, tools and judge); our absolute numbers aren’t comparable with the paper’s.

The agent-driven teams barely talked

The contrast with WorkBench

On WorkBench the same text team messaged in 95% of tasks: there, changes in the shared workspace collide, and the coordination sentence ties agreeing first to a concrete risk. Here nothing collides and every agent can do the whole search alone, so each one does. Splitting the collection, below, removes the second reason; the agents still didn’t talk.

How a team’s answer is chosen

With no messages, the agent-driven teams were in effect three agents searching in parallel, which is what independent is too. What differs is how the three answers become one.

  • Independent pastes all three answers together.The judge sees every worker’s answer, so a right answer anywhere can earn credit.
  • The first agent-driven runs kept whichever agent replied last.After re-grading, the text team ties independent at 4 of 10; both had the right answer somewhere on the team on 6 questions.
  • Agent-driven teams now vote when nobody submits a team answer.The vote only helps when two agents agree. In the split pilot below, all three answers differed on most questions, and the tie went to agent_1.

Splitting the collection between agents

To make sharing necessary, a pilot on the same 10 questions gave each agent only its own third of the 100,000 pages: a fixed random split, strict, so a page in a teammate’s third can’t be opened even by its id. Every agent was told who searches which part and that the evidence for a question is usually spread across all three. On the paper’s 100 questions the evidence pages span more than one third for 96, and the answer page sits in a single agent’s third for about half.

One run each, 10 questions. Team answer: the shared answer slot, else a majority vote over the agents’ final answers. Decentralized’s round summaries reach every worker automatically, and on one question that carried the answer across the split: only agent_3 could see the answer page, and by the third round two of the three workers had adopted its answer.

Why the agents didn’t communicate

Even split, with messages available at any time, the agents barely used them. Their traces (every model call, tool result and private reasoning step) show why. Counts are over the 30 agents in each team, 3 per question.

    How much to trust the judge

    • It is strict about formatting.It rejected a company name that added “Inc.” and, in two setups, a place name written with a comma instead of a semicolon. The lenient re-grade accepts the judge’s own extracted answer when it matches the gold answer up to case, punctuation and accents.
    • Our parser misread some of its verdicts, now fixed.The judge sometimes writes “Correctness: yes” instead of “correct: yes”, and our first parser read 2 such verdicts in the 100-question run as wrong. Every stored verdict on this page was re-read with the fixed parser.
    • The paper’s independent setup confuses it.Independent hands the judge all three workers’ answers pasted together. On 2 of the 10 questions one worker had the right answer and the judge extracted “None”.
    • It never accepted a wrong answer in the cases checked.Every case where the judge said yes and the string match said no was a spelling variant of the gold answer (accents, apostrophes).

    Problems found and fixed

    1. Agent-driven teams went idle before answering.WorkBench’s 12 model calls per wake-up ran out mid-research and nobody woke them. BrowseComp agents now get the single agent’s 30 calls in one go.
    2. Tool calls written out as text were taken as answers.gpt-oss sometimes writes a call as raw format tokens instead of making it. These now count as a tool error and the agent is asked to call again (BrowseComp only, so WorkBench runs stay comparable).
    3. Jobs on one node took each other’s embedding server.Each job’s embedder port was the next job’s chat-model port, so 4 of the 10 agent-driven jobs ran every search against the wrong model and found nothing. Ports are now even and setup checks the embedder; those 4 jobs were rerun and the broken runs set aside.
    4. Two correct answers were scored as wrong.The verdict parser missed the judge’s “Correctness: yes” wording. It now accepts it, and all stored verdicts were re-read; the 100-question run goes from 20 to 22 correct.
    5. Team answers were cut off before the answer line.The agent-driven team’s answer was built from each agent’s final reply as kept in the event log, which stores 800 characters. The paper’s format puts “Exact Answer” after the explanation, so about a third of agents’ final replies lost it, and 2–3 graded team answers per 10 questions had no answer in them. Teams now keep full replies, and the earlier runs were re-graded from the full replies saved in their traces.
    6. Too many runs for one interactive job’s memory.Each run holds its own copy of the collection, about 10 GB. Eight at once exceeded the 64 GB limit and two pilot runs were killed and rerun; plan about 12 GB per run.
    7. The 100-question single-agent run used older code.It started before the two fixes above that apply to it (the tool-free final answer and the retry of text tool calls), so it isn’t exactly comparable with the 10-question runs.

    Ways to make them communicate

    Candidates for the next runs, each aimed at one of the reasons above, on the same 10 questions and repeated so that results can be averaged.

    ReasonStrategy
    They thought they were donePropose, then commit. Agents can’t answer alone: each posts its candidate, confidence and key evidence; once all have posted, everyone sees the proposals, may check a teammate’s candidate in their own part, and the team commits.
    They couldn’t see what they were missingMake the split visible. Label search results “from your third; teammates search the rest”, and put the team framing next to the question.
    Teammates came to mind lateRegular check-ins. Every 8 or so calls, ask each agent to post anything teammates should know: candidates, ruled-out options, key facts. The agents still choose what to say.
    Nothing to share intoA shared notebook with fixed sections for candidates, facts and ruled-out options, read before searching.
    Each agent can still try aloneMake them depend on each other: fewer calls each, roles by clue (one finds candidates, the others check constraints), or different search tools per agent.
    Choosing to share at allAn automatic shared feed of every agent’s searches and top hits. A control: does seeing each other’s work help when nobody decides to share?

    Every strategy has to beat the free control above: taking the most confident agent’s answer, with no communication at all. Otherwise its gain comes from combining answers better, not from talking.
